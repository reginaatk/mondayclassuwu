How to Do a Kickflip

The classic flip trick: pop, flick, spin, catch. Learn it slowly, safely, and with control.

Best place to learn: smooth flat ground, away from traffic and obstacles

🛹Before you try a kickflip

A kickflip is a flip trick where your board spins once under you along its length while you stay centered and land back on the bolts. It builds directly on an ollie, so you should already feel comfortable riding, turning, stopping, and doing basic ollies on flat ground.

Skateboarding can cause falls and injuries. Always wear a helmet, and strongly consider wrist guards, knee pads, and elbow pads. Practice on flat, dry ground away from cars, people, and hard edges.

Recommended gear

  • Helmet: Properly fitted, buckled every time.
  • Pads: Wrist, knee, and elbow pads, especially while learning new tricks.
  • Shoes: Grippy skate shoes with a flat sole and decent side padding.
  • Board: A standard street deck with good grip tape and trucks you’re used to.
If you can’t comfortably roll and ollie without thinking about it, stay with those first. A solid foundation makes kickflips much safer and way less frustrating.

👣Foot placement and body position

Set up similar to an ollie, with a small adjustment in your front foot to help the board flip.

  • Back foot: On the tail, ball of your foot centered, ready to pop straight down.
  • Front foot: Just behind the front bolts, angled slightly (about 30–45°) toward the nose, with a bit of your toes hanging off the edge.
  • Shoulders: In line with the board, knees bent, weight centered—not leaning too far forward or back.

🔁Kickflip in 5 controlled phases

Think of the trick as a sequence: set up → pop → flick → wait → catch and land. Go slowly and focus on one part at a time.

  1. Set up and roll slowly
    Start at a comfortable, slow speed on smooth ground. Bend your knees slightly and keep your weight centered over the board.
  2. Pop like an ollie
    Press your back foot down sharply on the tail to pop the board. At the same time, begin to rise with your body—don’t stay stiff.
  3. Slide and flick the front foot
    As the board pops, slide your front foot up toward the nose at a slight diagonal toward the heel-side edge. When you reach the edge, flick out and slightly up with your front foot, not straight down. This flick starts the flip.
  4. Let the board flip under you
    After the flick, pull your front foot away and up so the board has space to spin. Keep your eyes on the grip tape—wait until you see it come back around.
  5. Catch and land
    When the board finishes its flip and the grip tape is facing up again, aim to catch it first with your back foot over the tail, then bring your front foot down over the front bolts. Bend your knees to absorb the landing and roll away.
Early on, it’s totally fine to practice the flick while holding onto a rail or wall, or even on grass or a carpeted surface so the board doesn’t roll away. Focus on a clean, controlled flip before worrying about rolling fast.

🧩Common problems and quick fixes

  • Board flips too much: You’re flicking too hard or too far off the side. Try a softer, shorter flick and keep more of your front foot on the board.
  • Board doesn’t flip enough: Make sure your front foot is angled and reaching the heel-side edge. Focus on a sharp flick off the corner of the nose.
  • Board flies behind you: You might be leaning back. Keep your chest over the middle of the board and pop straight down.
  • Landing with one foot: Commit with both feet over the bolts. Start at slower speed and tell yourself you’re landing with both feet, even if you have to step off right after.
Safety first

Smart practice habits

  • Warm up: A few minutes of rolling, turning, and basic ollies.
  • Short sessions: Take breaks—tired legs and frustration lead to sloppy, risky attempts.
  • Clear space: No loose boards, rocks, or people in your landing zone.
  • Know your limits: If something feels sketchy, reset your stance or slow down.
If you fall, take a moment to check in with your body. Stop if you feel pain, dizziness, or anything unusual, and seek medical help if needed.
Progression ideas

Build up gradually

  • Step 1: Practice the flick while holding a rail or wall.
  • Step 2: Try stationary kickflips on grass or carpet.
  • Step 3: Move to slow rolling attempts on smooth flat ground.
  • Step 4: Increase speed only when you’re landing consistently.
Control Consistency Then speed
Mindset

Dealing with frustration

Kickflips usually take many sessions to feel reliable. It’s normal to land one and then miss ten. Instead of chasing perfection, look for small wins: a cleaner flick, a straighter board, a better catch.

Set a simple goal like “10 focused attempts” and then stop. Ending before you’re exhausted keeps your confidence and your body safer.
