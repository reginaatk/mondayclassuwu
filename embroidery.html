Embroidery 101

Slow down, thread your needle, and learn the stitches that turn simple fabric into something personal.

First project idea: a tiny floral hoop with your initials

🧵What is embroidery?

Embroidery is the art of decorating fabric with needle and thread. It can be as simple as a single stitched initial on a napkin or as detailed as a full floral scene on a jacket. The magic comes from a handful of basic stitches, repeated with intention.

Embroidery rewards small, steady practice—five minutes of stitching a day beats one rushed weekend marathon.

Essential tools and materials

  • Fabric: Light to medium-weight cotton or linen is perfect for beginners.
  • Embroidery hoop: 5–7 inch wooden or plastic hoop to keep fabric drum-tight.
  • Needles: Embroidery/crewel needles (size 5–7 works well with 1–3 strands of floss).
  • Thread: Cotton embroidery floss, usually six strands you can separate.
  • Scissors: Small, sharp pair for clean cuts.
  • Marking tool: Water-soluble pen, heat-erasable pen, or light pencil for your design.

Set up your first hoop

  1. Separate the inner and outer rings of your hoop.
  2. Lay your fabric over the inner ring, then press the outer ring on top.
  3. Gently pull the fabric edges until it feels smooth and drum-tight.
  4. Tighten the screw, then tug the fabric once more to remove wrinkles.
  5. Trim excess fabric if it gets in your way (leave at least a few centimeters all around).

🪡Core stitches to learn first

You don’t need dozens of fancy techniques. Start with a small “toolbox” of stitches that each do something different: outline, fill, or add texture.

Running stitch A simple dashed line—great for borders, guidelines, and subtle texture.
Backstitch Creates a solid, ink-like line. Perfect for outlines, lettering, and stems.
Straight stitch Single stitches used for petals, leaves, rays, and filling small shapes.
Chain stitch Linked loops that form a decorative, slightly raised line—lovely for vines and borders.
Satin stitch Parallel stitches that fill a shape smoothly, like coloring with thread.
French knot Tiny raised dots—perfect for flower centers, berries, and texture.

How to work a simple backstitch

  1. Bring the needle up at point A (start of the line).
  2. Insert the needle down at point B (a short distance ahead).
  3. Come up again at point C (another stitch-length ahead of B).
  4. Insert the needle back down at point B, “back” into the end of the previous stitch.
  5. Repeat: up ahead, down into the previous endpoint, keeping your stitches even.

🌸Your first mini project

Try a small 10–12 cm hoop with your initial and a few flowers. It’s quick, forgiving, and teaches you how different stitches work together.

Suggested stitch plan

  • Initial: Outline the letter with backstitch.
  • Stem: Use backstitch or chain stitch for a curved line.
  • Leaves: Straight stitches radiating from a center line.
  • Flowers: Straight stitches for petals, French knots for centers.
Don’t chase perfection. Uneven stitches are just “handmade” showing through—and that’s the charm.
Quick reference

Thread & needle basics

Most embroidery floss comes in 6 strands. For beginners:

  • 1–2 strands: Fine lines and small details.
  • 3 strands: Everyday outlines and petals.
  • 4–6 strands: Bold lines and chunky texture.
Cut thread lengths around 30–45 cm. Longer pieces tangle more easily and wear out faster.
Stitch chooser

Match stitch to effect

  • Clean outline: Backstitch or split stitch.
  • Dashed line: Running stitch.
  • Soft filled shape: Satin stitch.
  • Textured border: Chain stitch or blanket stitch.
  • Little dots: French knots.

Common beginner mistakes

And how to avoid them

  • Fabric too loose: Tighten the hoop until it feels like a drum.
  • Thread knots: Use shorter lengths and let the needle dangle to untwist.
  • Puckering: Don’t pull too hard—aim for snug, not tight.
  • Messy back: Finish threads with small knots or weave them under existing stitches.
